# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Stack

Single self-contained `index.html` (inline SVG, CSS, vanilla JS). Confirmed with the user: no build step, no dependencies, opens directly in a browser.

## Users

A visitor exploring a fictional North Galician island weather station — an observer's instrument board presented as an exhibit. The job is to read and scrub a simulated week of weather through analogue instruments, to watch the storm build and pass, and to understand the station's story.

## Product Purpose

A self-contained meteorological display that dramatises a passing depression through brass-and-enamel analogue instruments with mechanical needle inertia. Success: the visitor immediately reads a Victorian-scientific instrument board, can scrub time, sees the storm react across the whole board, and can enlarge any dial for exact readings and history.

## Positioning

No rectangles of numbers — every value is a dial. Needle physics (damped springs with per-instrument character) and a board-wide storm reaction are the mechanism a static dashboard cannot copy.

## Operating Context

Viewed in a browser on desktop or phone. Accelerated time at ~1.8 s per simulated hour, looping a simulated week. Interactions: drag the brass time lever, arrow keys, hold/auto switch, hover for engraved tooltip, click dial/plate to enlarge, tap the barometer glass.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- All data is fictional and generated in-page from a smooth simulated weather system.
- Instruments: aneroid barometer (with draggable set-hand), anemometer + cup spinner, 32-point wind rose, dry/wet thermometer pair + humidity sub-dial, rain gauge (daily 09:00 drain), barograph (rotating drum, 3-day curved trace, 7-day enlarged view), sunshine recorder, tide dial, sea state and visibility dials.
- Header: 24-hour station clock with day-of-week hand; signal mast in baize-lined porthole with storm cone.
- Observer's log and typeset 12-hour general synopsis below the board.
- Responsive: 4-column board → 2 columns → single column (barometer first). Reduced-motion path required.
- Palette, materials, maker name and footer credit are pinned by the brief.

## Brand Commitments

Station: "Sofia Perez-Vigo (Station 25-09)", invented North Galician island, Spain. Title plaque: "SOFIA PEREZ-VIGO · METEOROLOGICAL STATION · EST. 2026". Instrument maker: "Tarrant & Lyle, Leith". Footer: "Designed by Hugo Perez-Vigo". Mood: steadfast, maritime, Victorian-scientific; calm even when the weather is not.

## Evidence on Hand

None; all content is authored as clearly labelled fictional/simulated material.

## Product Principles

- Everything is a dial; no grids of numeric cards.
- Motion has mechanical character (inertia, stickiness, hunting, gusts), not decoration.
- The storm is a single coordinated event across board, mast, glass, log and lighting.
- Simulated data must be smooth, plausible and unmistakably fictional.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Reduced-motion path (direct needles, static drum with travelling pen). Keyboard-operable time lever. Legible engraved-style serif with adequate contrast on enamel.
